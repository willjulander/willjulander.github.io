Case study

Daisies for Mercy

A 3D-printed daisy pin that helps a hospital honor its best nurses — designed, produced, and delivered by one person with one printer.

StatusIn production
SinceFeb 2025
CustomerMercy Hospital
Units served2

Overview

Mercy Hospital in Iowa runs the DAISY Award — a recognition program where nurses honor coworkers for extraordinary care. They needed a physical token to go with it: something small, durable, and personal enough that a name could be attached to each one.

I design and print daisy pins for that. Each pin starts as a CAD model, gets printed in matte PLA on a Bambu Lab printer, and is delivered to the hospital in person. Nurses put a coworker's name on a daisy to honor them.

What makes this project matter to me isn't the geometry — a daisy is a simple part. It's that the whole pipeline is mine: design, printability, production, quoting, delivery, and payment. Two hospital units — Postpartum and the NICU — currently hand out these daisies, and growing that is ongoing work.

Process

  1. Model the daisy in CAD. The petal profile went through several revisions to look right and still print cleanly face-down without supports.
  2. Slice and tune. Set up in Bambu Studio for matte PLA — dialing in the settings that make a batch reliable, not just one good print.
  3. Print in batches. Producing enough at a time to keep units stocked without wasting material on failed plates.
  4. Deliver and manage. Quote the order, print it, walk it into the hospital, and handle payment. Repeat as units reorder.

What I learned

A part that leaves your desk teaches you things a school assignment can't: designing for the person who receives it, making production repeatable, and being the one accountable when a batch has to be right. It's a small product — but it's a real one, working in a real hospital.
